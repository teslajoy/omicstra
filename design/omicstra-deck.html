Slide 2 · what I set out to build

Original vision

Omicstra was not about finding the best AI model. It was about building a multi-agent MCP server where scientists could ask biological questions like:

“Where is immune checkpoint signalling active?” a cellular program
“Find regions that look like tertiary lymphoid structures.” an anatomical structure
“Show me stromal remodelling.” ambiguous - program or tissue state?

Specialised AI agents coordinate computational methods. But they do not decide blindly: they reason over evidence about which method is appropriate for the biological question.

Keep these three in mind. By slide 10 they route to three different methods, and that is the finding.

Slide 4 · the answer to “why so many embeddings”

From tissue to a biological answer

Two measurements, three representations. gpath2vec is derived from the same counts Novae encodes, so it is a different level of abstraction over one measurement, not an independent survey. Guardrails are a decision over evidence, with three outcomes. Refusal is the part no leaderboard has.
Slide 5 · the evaluation

So I stepped back

Instead of building the MCP first, I evaluated multiple alignment methods on 92 triple-negative breast cancer patients, using three biological representations.

The goal: learn how these different views relate.

The unit is the niche, not the spot - spot-level cross-patient retrieval collapsed to chance, which is why it moved. The + node: the two molecular encoders are concatenated into one 576-d vector and enter as one side; imaging enters as the other. Imaging and molecular are never concatenated with each other. On 'only H&E enters': true for deployment and the headline z_he view; retrieval is the evaluation and necessarily scores both sides.
Slide 6 · the turn

What surprised me

I expected one model to win. Instead, different methods were good at different biology.

The run that pairs best is close to worst at grouping. The runs that group well carry almost no pathway signal to patients they were not trained on.

No method occupies a corner.

There wasn't a single winner.

R4 AUC 0.859 / ARI 0.101. R5 0.246, R6 0.244, R1 0.234 on ARI. R6 carries ZERO of four pathways cross-patient. Careful claim: R4 is the only CONTRASTIVE run carrying all four - B1, B2 and B3 also reach 4/4, and R4 beats them on magnitude, about 1.5x on every pathway. Do not say 'the only run'.
Slide 7 · why “evidence-based” means something

And one method was disqualified

One classical method passed both axes on the previous slide. It was scoring by encoding which patient the sample came from.

How it is computed

Two permutation z-scores on the same space, 10,000 label shuffles each. The first asks how much more similar niches are when they share a tumour immune microenvironment class. The second asks the same for patient identity. The ratio is the first over the second.

Below the floor means the space is reading provenance, not biology. Without this diagnostic B1 would have been selected.

This is structural in any study where several regions come from one patient.

The ratio is two permutation z-scores on the same space, 10,000 label shuffles each: how much more similar niches are when they share a tumour immune microenvironment class, over how much more similar they are when they share a patient. Slide 9 · the original vision, now grounded

What the MCP becomes

Most AI systems:   question  ->  the AI chooses a tool

omicstra:
1Scientist asks “Where are checkpoint pathways active?”
2Agent understands the question intent, not keyword matching
3Routes to the method shown to answer that question best and records a tie, or refuses, when the evidence does not separate candidates
4Returns the result - plus why that method was selected the measurement, and whether a rule, a model or a human made the call

Instead of an AI agent guessing, it consults evidence and can explain why.

Step 3 is the one that matters: routes on evidence, records a tie, or refuses. Step 4 is the reproducibility claim as a number - every decision labelled rule, model or human.
Slide 10 · back to where we started

The three questions, resolved

Same two assays, same data, three destinations. B4 is the counter-intuitive one: the best method for finding TLS-like regions is an untrained projection, and contrastive training makes it worse (z = 25.5). Expect a question there. Slide 11 · durability

Why I think this matters

As new foundation models appear - better pathology models, better transcriptomic models, protein models, multimodal models -

1

we don't rebuild the system

the graph, the guards and the evaluation surface stay the same

2

we evaluate them

the same H1 / H2 / H3 tests, on the same cohort, against the same floors

3

we update the routing table

a new method has to beat every existing route to be admitted

The system improves without being rebuilt, because it accumulates what is known about which computational methods answer which biological questions.

A new method is admitted only if it beats EVERY existing route, not just the incumbent.
Slide 12 · how far this travels

The framework generalises. The evaluation doesn't.

These are different claims, and only one of them is free. The system knows which is which, because every criterion declares whether it was derived here or holds anywhere.

What generalises

  • the biological questions
  • the evaluation framework
  • the routing architecture
  • the guardrails

What does not, automatically

  • which alignment method wins
  • the thresholds
  • pathway significance
  • cohort-specific calibrations

A new cohort does not inherit a threshold. It escalates, and someone has to derive it or adopt it deliberately.

This is the answer to "does R4 win in pancreas too?" The honest answer is unknown, and the system escalates rather than assuming. That is a stronger position than a confident yes.
One thing to be clear about

None of this predicts outcome.

Spatially localising checkpoint and stromal programs from archived material is a precondition for morphology-based stratification, not an instance of it. That needs a cohort with outcomes attached, prospectively.

“I thought I was building an AI system for multimodal biology. The real challenge wasn't building another alignment model - it was understanding what each method is actually good at biologically. That shifted Omicstra into an evidence-based reasoning layer that helps an AI agent select the right computational method for the biological question being asked, and explain why. I think that's a more reusable piece of infrastructure, because it can keep incorporating new models as the field evolves.”
